# Wave 1D: Cross-paper positioning audit

## 1. Parent-paper positioning

The paper positions itself accurately as closing the asymmetry the README names. The introduction makes the framing explicit: "The amendment lifecycle has been formalized on the first half... The response side has been left to the admission predicate, which means an enforcement act has had no positive type-level discipline of its own: the only thing the type system enforced was that the act admitted, not that the act terminated, reverted, or carried a counterparty's signature." Section 2 background carries the same shape and names the four parent primitives that the construction depends on (treaty intersection, ladder-floor stability, amendment refinement bridge, essential-predicate chain). Section 3 substrate calls itself "the response-side analog of the parent paper's amendment trajectory." The novelty claim is bounded to the response side and the composition theorem; the paper does not pretend to re-derive the amendment side. The framing is honest.

## 2. Bilateral-protocol positioning

The paper engages the bilateral construction concretely at one specific point and uses the term loosely elsewhere. The concrete engagement is in section 3, "Bilateral envelopes on destructive variants": "A destructive action's admission requires a bilateral DSSE envelope over the canonical request body. The device-polity signature and the operator-polity signature must be drawn from independent keys; the parent paper's strict bilateral DSSE verifier rejects envelopes with reused signer keys, wrong predicate types, and noncanonical payloads." This points at the bilateral DSSE verifier and names three of the rejection codes from the sibling paper's verifier (reused signer, wrong predicate type, noncanonical payload). Elsewhere ("bilateral predicate intersection", "bilateral-cosignature reduction"), the paper uses the bilateral vocabulary without naming the six gates or the five rejection codes that the bilateral paper introduces. The reduction is bound to the parent's treaty intersection theorem rather than to the sibling paper's DSSE verifier, which is the right call: the bilateral paper is the wire-level protocol, this paper consumes its abstraction. The paper does not cite the bilateral paper by name (anonymized for review is the polite convention), and the substrate description names the parent's verifier file path rather than the bilateral paper itself, which is reasonable.

## 3. DEA-paper relationship

The two papers use the typed rollback witness consistently. This paper carries TTL invariant plus action-class tag plus optional rollback receipt at the constructor; the DEA paper applies the same structural shape to constitutional emergency powers. Section 7 discussion of this paper engages the emergency-powers framing directly: "real emergency powers are notable for the absence of reliable rollback. A government that invokes emergency authority does not in practice undo the actions taken during the emergency; the very property the TTL discipline formalizes is the property emergency-powers regimes have struggled to guarantee." This places the endpoint-security application as a stricter discipline than what emergency-powers scholarship can guarantee in the constitutional case. The cross-reference is appropriately one-directional: this paper does not cite the DEA paper, and the DEA paper would naturally cite the parent rather than this sibling. The cross-paper coherence holds.

## 4. ATS-paper relationship

The §7 "Agentic-AI oversight" paragraph is the place where overlap is most plausible. The paragraph stays at the right level: it names the bounded-action invariant, the bilateral-cosignature reduction, and the Anthropic alignment-evaluation literature as upstream motivation, and frames the construction as the downstream substrate. It does not develop the LLM tool-call mapping or the five-component decomposition (authorization, subject, effect, lifetime, reversal expectation) that the ATS paper carries. The agentic-AI thread is positioned as a forward-looking gesture inside a paper whose primary application is endpoint security: "A typed substrate that refuses to construct an executive action without a TTL witness and an action class tag is a primitive other safety systems can cite without adopting the substrate as a whole." That is the right voice. The overlap with ATS is at the level of shared substrate, not duplicated content.

## 5. Related-work chapter

Section 8 covers four of the requested literatures: compensating transactions and sagas; capability revocation (EROS, seL4, Capsicum); endpoint detection and response (CrowdStrike Falcon, SentinelOne, Microsoft Defender for Endpoint, Sublime); and the verified-systems pattern (AWS Cedar, SampCert). It adds provenance/lineage and an agentic-AI safety paragraph. The strongest paragraph is the capability-revocation one: it names three specific systems, identifies the discipline they share, and articulates the structural lift to the cross-organizational boundary ("The OS-capability literature solves revocation at one kernel; the construction here gates revocation at the receipt-graph boundary between kernels"). The weakest paragraph is the verified-systems one: it cites Cedar and SampCert without engaging the longer Isabelle / Coq / Lean systems-verification line (CompCert, seL4 the verification effort distinct from the capability work, Iron, Project Everest, IronFleet), which a USENIX Security related-work reviewer would expect. Reversible-computing scholarship (Bennett's reversible Turing machine work, the Pendulum architecture, R, Janus) is entirely absent and a reviewer will notice given the paper's title.

## 6. Anthropic outreach signaling

The paper does not signal the outreach in the body. The introduction and section 7 discuss agentic-AI oversight in third-person terms naming primitives that "other safety systems can cite," not first-person invitations to a particular research group. Section 2 background and section 7 discussion both reference "Anthropic alignment-evaluation literature on alignment-faking and situational-awareness" as upstream motivation, which is a legitimate citation pattern; this is different from signaling co-authorship interest. The README's deferral discipline is correctly enforced in the prose.

## 7. Submission readiness telegraphing

The paper telegraphs its own immaturity in one specific way that is honest rather than performative. The introduction names the evaluation as measuring "the four reversible paths against fixture corpora" and naming "the destructive class, the missing TTL scheduler, and the stub endpoint sensor as deployment gaps the measurement does not cover." The contribution list explicitly carries "deployment gaps" as an outcome. The abstract is similarly disciplined: "the bilateral-destructive variant is reported as a deployment obligation rather than a measurement." This is the right register for a draft, and a USENIX Security reviewer would read it as honest scoping rather than as a tell that the paper is unfinished. The composition-theorem framing is overconfident in one place ("The load-bearing claim is a composition theorem stating that..."): the README acknowledges that the load-bearing candidate is only "plausibly non-`rfl`" and the abstract should match that hedge. The body's framing ("plausibly", "candidate") is appropriately tentative.

## Bottom line

The paper reads coherently as the sibling to the parent, not a derivative restatement. It positions itself against the bilateral paper by consuming its abstraction rather than re-deriving it, against the DEA paper by naming the emergency-powers framing as partially apt and partially misleading, and against the ATS paper by keeping the agentic-AI thread to a forward-looking gesture inside an endpoint-security paper. The Anthropic-outreach discipline holds. The submission-readiness telegraphing is honest about the missing scheduler and the missing destructive-class executors. Two correctable gaps remain: the abstract overstates the headline theorem's certainty relative to the body's hedge, and the related-work chapter is missing the broader systems-verification line and the reversible-computing literature that the title implies.
