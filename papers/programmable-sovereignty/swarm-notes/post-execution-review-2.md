### Issue 1: Three tables and three figures are labeled but never \ref'd in prose
**Severity**: major
**Where**: `sections/03-substrate.tex:44` (`fig:admission`); `sections/04-model.tex:80` (`fig:treaty`), `:88` (`fig:amendment`); `sections/05-implementation.tex:8` (`tab:artifacts`), `:57` (`tab:theorems`); `sections/06-evaluation.tex:10` (`tab:evaluation`).
**Problem**: All three figures and three of the four tables have `\label{}` directives but no `Figure~\ref{}` or `Table~\ref{}` ever appears in body prose. Only `tab:assumptions` is referenced (twice). A reader of §3 sees the admission-hook figure as a free-floating illustration; §4's bilateral-DSSE prose at lines 38-45 never says "see Figure~\ref{fig:treaty}"; §4's amendment paragraph at 67-72 doesn't point at `fig:amendment`; the implementation-artifact table and the proof-to-code map both sit in §5 with no in-text pointers. ACM SIG style and venue-style guides expect every float to be cited from prose; the absence reads as either (a) figures inserted last and never woven into the argument, or (b) labels redundant. Either way it's a coherence problem a reviewer will flag.
**Suggested fix**: Add one sentence-internal pointer per float: §3's "Admission hook" paragraph gains "(Figure~\ref{fig:admission})" at the malformed-context-denial mention; §4's treaty paragraph references `fig:treaty` after introducing the bilateral DSSE binding; §4's amendment paragraph references `fig:amendment` after the type-conditioned enactment sentence; §5's "treaty primitive" paragraph references `tab:artifacts` and the proof-inventory paragraph references `tab:theorems`; §6 opening references `tab:evaluation` once.

### Issue 2: Compound Proposal 289 dollar figure inconsistent between prose and bib note; "passed" elides the negotiated rescission
**Severity**: minor
**Where**: `sections/02-background.tex:9` (prose); `bib.bib` entry `compoundProposal289` (note field)
**Problem**: §2 prose says "the 2024 Proposal 289 attack passed a \$24M extraction proposal". The bib entry's `note` field reads `"Proposed July 24 2024; carried 682,191 / 633,636 (52% margin); later rescinded by negotiated settlement"` and the `howpublished` URL points at the protos.com retrospective titled "Compound DAO asleep at the wheel as \$25M governance attack passes". The prose figure ($24M) and the URL figure ($25M) disagree, and the prose verb "passed" omits the negotiated rescission that the bib note records. A hostile reviewer searching for the proposal number will find the actual extraction amount listed at $24M in some sources and $25M in others (it was a 499,000 COMP transfer ≈ $24M at the proposal date), so the choice is defensible - but the inconsistency with the bib entry's own note is not. The "passed" verb is also doing rhetorical work it shouldn't: the proposal passed the on-chain vote but was rescinded before execution.
**Suggested fix**: Pick one figure (the proposal moved 499,000 COMP; choose $24M and state it in both the prose and the bib note), and qualify "passed" with "before later rescission by negotiated settlement" - the threat-of-token-weighted-enactment claim survives the qualification.

### Issue 6: Threat model omits the colluding-cosigners adversary class
**Severity**: major
**Where**: `sections/04-model.tex:60` (threat-model paragraph); `sections/09-limitations.tex` (no bullet)
**Problem**: §4 threat model enumerates adversary capabilities (control the agent process, malformed args, replay, omit evidence, reorder JSON, smuggle trust roots, operate honest-looking non-admitted polity, inject pins). It does not name the case where both kernels in a bilateral treaty are jointly malicious - for example, two vendor kernels operated by the same legal entity colluding to manufacture cross-vendor evidence the buyer's verifier admits as bilateral. The bilateral-DSSE strict verifier requires two independent signer keys (per `bilateral_dsse.rs:996`), but key independence is not party independence; the same actor controlling both signing keys produces evidence indistinguishable from a real bilateral admission. The substrate's whole "two-of-two cosigning" security property collapses to "one-of-one" under that adversary. §9 has no bullet acknowledging this. A senior PC reviewer with crypto-protocol background will flag this within the first reading - it is the single highest-impact attack the threat model hand-waves past, since §3 explicitly leans on "joint authorization intent" as the missing primitive that distinguishes Chiodos from generic in-toto provenance.
**Suggested fix**: Add one sentence to the §4 threat model paragraph naming the colluding-signers attack (e.g., "Two kernels operated by a single actor are out of scope for the bilateral-DSSE primitive, which establishes joint signing intent across two key pairs but not across two distinct legal/operational principals; party-independence is an operational discipline assumption, not a cryptographic one") and a parallel §9 bullet under operational discipline. Alternatively, name `kernel_independence_attestation` (TEE-rooted or witness-attested) as the v2 substrate primitive that promotes key independence to party independence.

### Issue 7: §6 reproducibility paragraph never tells a reader how to invoke `CHIO_SOURCE`
**Severity**: minor
**Where**: `sections/06-evaluation.tex:6`
**Problem**: §6 says "Hand-written harnesses compile temporary Rust projects against the source tree referenced by `CHIO_SOURCE`; the dispatch and replay scripts redirect `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` into a temporary directory so measurement does not mutate the source tree." A reader who wants to reproduce the numbers in Table 2 has no instruction on what to set `CHIO_SOURCE` to. The pre-fix `bench/run-dispatch-allow.sh` script defaulted `CHIO_SOURCE` to an author-machine checkout and fell back to `$ROOT` if that path was absent - none of which was documented in §6. A reviewer attempting an artifact-evaluation walk-through stops at the first script. Also: the path prefix to the scripts (`papers/programmable-sovereignty/bench/run-*.sh`) is not stated; only the script basenames appear in Table 2.
**Suggested fix**: Add one sentence after the `CHIO_SOURCE` mention naming the expected value ("set `CHIO_SOURCE` to a Chio workspace checkout; the script defaults to the repository root if unset") and prefix the scripts in Table 2 with `papers/programmable-sovereignty/bench/` so a reader can find them from the repo root. Optionally add an artifact-evaluation appendix-style sentence pointing at the README for end-to-end instructions.

### Issue 8: Abstract toggles between "three-party buyer-vendor closure" and "three-vendor demonstration" within four lines
**Severity**: nit
**Where**: `paper.tex:27` (abstract)
**Problem**: The abstract's third-to-last clause says "demonstrate the construction with a three-party buyer-vendor closure backed by replay fixtures" - and the next sentence says "the three-vendor demonstration replays both admitted and denied paths". "Three-party" and "three-vendor" are not synonyms (three vendors plus a buyer is four parties; three parties in a buyer-vendor closure is one buyer plus two vendors), and the rest of the paper uniformly uses "three-vendor". A skim-reader who counts parties finds the abstract internally inconsistent.
**Suggested fix**: Use "three-vendor" in both places ("a three-vendor buyer-closure backed by replay fixtures" matches §1 bullet 4 and §5/§6 prose). The contribution is unchanged.
