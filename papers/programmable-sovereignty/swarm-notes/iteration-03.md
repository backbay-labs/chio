Her objection lands in print regardless of what the project does. The only choice is whether it lands as a co-launched response paper (project-defined frame: "Walch's critique anchors the limits the substrate must respect") or as a citation in someone else's hit-piece six months later (project-loses-frame). Cost: one private email, 14 days, willingness to weaken one sentence in S7. Payoff: every subsequent reviewer reads Chio through the Walch-response lens, not the Walch-attack lens. This dominates the other strategic questions on the list because it is the only one irrevocably foreclosed by pushing the arXiv button.

Provocateur agent (round-3) was right; the short paper is the wedge, not the consolation prize.

Co-authorship dominates co-signing because it forces internal Anthropic review (free hostile-read) and forecloses the rename question. DeepMind and OpenAI are second-tier; Anthropic is uniquely positioned by name collision plus RSP framing.

The Tornado Cash precedent (polish-diff round 2) is exactly the failure mode this pre-Slack catches early.

## Persona X: Two-paper comparator (Path C verdict)

**Verdict: (B) Conditional split.** Each paper *can* stand alone, but only if four specific changes are made; without them, the split is a salami slice that weakens both. With them, the short paper is the stronger artifact of the two.

**Q1: Orthogonal claim, or strict subset?** Strict subset, today. The bilateral-DSSE construction *is* the runtime expression of treaty intersection (the long paper's load-bearing theorem `treaty_admission_iff_predicate_intersection`). The short-paper abstract says "two parties jointly admitted an action under a treaty-bound predicate"; the long-paper abstract says "treaty intersection composes with ladder-floor reduction without changing admission." Same admission decision, two framings. To escape the salami charge the short paper needs one claim the long paper does not foreground: the five-code rejection taxonomy as a *standalone audit-protocol contribution* (which the long paper buries in implementation). The short paper's S1 already gestures at this with "five rejection codes distinguish each failure class so downstream audit can identify which constitutional precondition failed" -- promote that to the headline contribution; the long paper does not advertise it.

**Q2: Does the short-paper abstract stand alone?** Mostly yes, with one tell. The abstract is self-contained on what the primitive does and what it defeats. The tell is the closing clause: "the companion position paper defends the full polity, amendment, and Lean-attestable construction." That sentence advertises the long paper and asks the reader to accept that *the formal backing lives elsewhere*. A short-paper PC will read this as "the proofs are not in this paper" -- which is fine if the cryptographic claim does not depend on them, but S4 (Formal Sketch) is a stub. Drafted honestly, S4 either proves the verifier's accept-set theorem freestanding (drop the companion clause) or admits it depends on the companion (the salami charge bites).

**Q3: Is the four-concerns composition the genuinely novel artifact?** No -- the iteration-01 PL skeptic correctly identified canonical-bytes subject-digest binding as the load-bearing primitive. The four concerns (local enforcement, treaty co-signing, strict rejection, BBS presentation) are protocol *architecture*, valuable but not novel: SLSA + in-toto + DSSE + BBS already exists as a composition pattern. The novelty is what S2 line 9 names: "subject digest binds the two receipts plus the treaty's scope, ladder, and continuation state" -- binding *continuation hash* (linking to receipt-graph state) into the subject digest is the move no prior supply-chain primitive makes. S2 should lead with that single binding, not with the four-concerns decomposition.

**Q4: Cross-citation discipline against self-plagiarism desk-reject?** Defensible only with the README's separability claim *enforced in the artifact*. Today S4 is a stub and S2 already gestures at the long paper. Make the rule mechanical: short-paper sections cite the long paper exactly twice (S1 for context, S9 for the polity/amendment scope it disclaims), never as load-bearing dependency. The Lean theorems live in the long paper *and* the short paper carries a self-contained formal sketch (an accept-set characterization theorem in S4 that does not import any polity machinery). If both go to the same PC, submit with a cover note naming artifact-disjointness: long paper owns `Intersection.lean`'s amendment theorems; short paper owns the verifier-accept-set theorem.

Highest-priority follow-up: draft S4 of the short paper with a single theorem whose statement and proof do not reference the polity triple $(T,C,K)$, amendments, or Hart -- the verifier's accept-set as the intersection of canonical-bytes equality, dual independent signature verification, predicate-type match, lease freshness, and subject-digest equality -- because until S4 exists the short paper *is* a salami slice citing the long paper for formal backing.
