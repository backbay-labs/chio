# TEE Attestation vs. Sensor-Grounded Admission: Mapping the Irreducible Delta

Research note R-2 for the sensor-grounded-admission paper, May 2026. No paper-file edits attempted; this is a read-only survey of primary TEE attestation specifications. The question driving the survey: does any published TEE attestation product or specification already produce evidence that satisfies the sensor-grounded admission paper's three structural commitments (per-sensor `dropped_event_count` / `deadline_miss_count` in evidence, predicate-on-coverage rather than binary verdict, and a wire-level distinction between "sensor degraded" and "sensor never installed")?

## 1. Top finding

No surveyed TEE attestation product or specification carries the three commitments. Every surveyed wire format (Intel TDX Quote v4 / v5, AMD SEV-SNP `ATTESTATION_REPORT` v2, Apple PCC `AttestationBundle`, Arm CCA realm token, AWS Nitro attestation document, NVIDIA H100 device attestation report, Microsoft Azure Attestation TDX EAT profile) treats evidence as a hash chain of code/firmware loaded into the trust domain plus a small fixed user-defined nonce slot. Runtime measurement registers (TDX RTMR0-3, CCA REM, Nitro PCR8) are SHA-384 extension chains: they record the existence and order of measured events but cannot expose structured per-sensor coverage state, drop counts, deadline misses, or installed-vs-never-installed distinctions to a verifier. The irreducible delta survives intact.

## 2. Intel TDX

The Intel TDX Module Base Architecture Specification, document 348549-002US, January 2023 (TDX 1.5), defines two measurement register families in Section 12.1: a build-time MRTD (SHA-384 of the launch image) and four RTMR registers extended by SHA-384 at runtime. RTMR semantics are stated explicitly in Section 12.1.2: `RTMR[i] = SHA384(RTMR[i] || x)`, with "typical expected usage [...] for TPM emulation during guest TD OS secure boot by the VBIOS". The TDREPORT_STRUCT (Section 12.2, Figure 12.1) carries: TEE_TCB_INFO, REPORTMACSTRUCT, TDINFO_STRUCT, REPORTDATA (64 bytes user-defined). TDINFO_STRUCT carries MRTD, MRCONFIGID, MROWNER, MROWNERCONFIG, RTMR0-3.

The verifier-facing Quote format is specified in the Intel TDX DCAP Quote Generation and Verification Library API, Rev 0.9, May 2025, Section A.3.2 (v4 Quote Body) and A.4.4 (v5 Quote Body for TDX 1.5). Both formats carry the same fields: MRTD, MRCONFIGID, MROWNER, MROWNERCONFIG, RTMR0-3, REPORTDATA. Section 2.3.2 documents the verifier's contract for RTMR semantics: by convention RTMR[0] and RTMR[1] are filled by the TD virtual firmware, RTMR[2] by the OS, and "for RTMR[3], contact the TD workload owner." There is no normative claim about per-sensor health, drop counts, or deadline misses, and no structured way to express "sensor degraded" versus "sensor never installed" within a 48-byte SHA-384 register.

Intel's developer article "Runtime Integrity Measurement and Attestation in a Trust Domain" extends IMA into RTMR via a new `ima_rtmr_extend` function: kernel-loaded code, command-line options, initrd and ACPI tables are extended into RTMR0-1. This pushes attestation coverage from launch into runtime, but the quantity attested is still a chain of measured code hashes. It does not introduce per-sensor `dropped_event_count` or `deadline_miss_count` semantics; degradation of a runtime sensor produces, structurally, the same RTMR digest as no sensor having been installed in the first place.

## 3. AMD SEV-SNP

The AMD SEV Secure Nested Paging Firmware ABI Specification, publication 56860, Revision 1.54, November 2022, Table 21, defines the `ATTESTATION_REPORT` structure version 2. Fields (with byte offsets): VERSION, GUEST_SVN, POLICY, FAMILY_ID, IMAGE_ID, VMPL, SIGNATURE_ALGO, CURRENT_TCB, PLATFORM_INFO, SIGNING_KEY / MASK_CHIP_KEY / AUTHOR_KEY_EN bitfield, REPORT_DATA (64 bytes user-defined), MEASUREMENT (SHA-384 of guest at launch, calculated by `SNP_LAUNCH_FINISH`), HOST_DATA, ID_KEY_DIGEST, AUTHOR_KEY_DIGEST, REPORT_ID, REPORT_ID_MA, REPORTED_TCB, CHIP_ID, COMMITTED_TCB, CURRENT_BUILD / MINOR / MAJOR, COMMITTED_BUILD / MINOR / MAJOR, LAUNCH_TCB, SIGNATURE. Revisions 1.55 (April 2024), 1.56 (October 2024), 1.57 (January 2025), and 1.58 (May 2025) add `Turin` TCB fields and a `Mitigation Status` section but do not extend the wire format with per-sensor runtime telemetry.

The structural shape: MEASUREMENT is launch-only, computed by `SNP_LAUNCH_FINISH` as documented in Section 2 of the spec. Once finalized, the firmware-attested measurement does not change for the life of the guest. There is no RTMR-equivalent. Runtime evidence about the guest's posture lives entirely in the 64-byte REPORT_DATA field, which is opaque to the platform: the guest is free to put a hash there, but the firmware does not interpret it, the verifier has no schema to parse it, and the platform certifies only that the same guest signed REPORT_DATA together with the launch measurement. Per-sensor coverage cannot be expressed in this slot at any depth a verifier would interpret as a predicate.

## 4. Apple Private Cloud Compute and Platform Secure Attestation

Apple's open-source `security-pcc` repository (release dated 10/02/2024) carries the canonical `AttestationBundle` protobuf at `CloudAttestation/CloudAttestation/Proto/AttestationBundle.proto`. The bundle fields are: `sep_attestation` (DER-encoded Secure Enclave attestation), `ap_ticket` (Image4 manifest of the boot chain), `sealed_hashes` (`SealedHashLedger`, a map of named slots, each a sequence of `SealedHash.Entry` records keyed to AppleKeyStore Software Sealed Hash Registers), `provisioning_certificate_chain` (DCIK chain), `app_data` (use-case specific bytes, hashed into the attestation nonce), `key_expiration`, `transparency_proofs` (proofs of inclusion in the public transparency log), `local_policy` (boot policy), `sfr_manifest` (firmware and restore manifest). The `SealedHashLedger.Entry` type carries `flags`, `digest` (up to 48 bytes ratcheted into a Software Sealed Hash Register), and a tagged-union `info` field of `Cryptex`, `Cryptex.Salt`, or `SecureConfig`.

Apple's deeper runtime attestation goes farther than TDX in coverage because the Sealed Hash Ledger records measured cryptex loads and secure-config writes after boot, and because the transparency log binds every measurement back to a publicly inspectable software image. But the quantity attested is still the chain of measured software images and configuration loads. There is no per-sensor `dropped_event_count`, no `deadline_miss_count`, no installed-vs-never-installed indicator. PCC's threat model treats degraded telemetry as an implementation concern of the workload, not a load-bearing primitive in the wire format the verifier evaluates.

The Apple Security Research blog post "Private Cloud Compute: a new frontier for AI privacy in the cloud" (June 2024) describes the attestation goal as "user devices will be willing to send data only to PCC nodes that can cryptographically attest to running publicly listed software" - i.e., the predicate is membership in the transparency log, not coverage of runtime sensors.

## 5. Microsoft Azure Attestation

The Azure Attestation TDX EAT profile, published at `learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/attestation/trust-domain-extensions-eat-profile`, page version dated 2026-02-27, enumerates exactly the claims a TDX EAT issues. The TDX claims are the seven SHA-384 measurement-register hashes from Intel's spec (`tdx_mrtd`, `tdx_mrconfigid`, `tdx_mrowner`, `tdx_mrownerconfig`, `tdx_rtmr0`, `tdx_rtmr1`, `tdx_rtmr2`, `tdx_rtmr3`), TDX module measurements (`tdx_mrseam`, `tdx_mrsignerseam`), TD attributes, XFAM, REPORT_DATA, and an `attester_tcb_status` string. Microsoft adds an `x-ms-runtime` claim, defined as "JSON object containing 'claims' that are defined and generated within the attested environment [...] specifically formatted as a UTF-8 encoding of well formed JSON" - a free-form container of enclave-held data that the platform does not interpret. MAA is the broker that signs the EAT; the platform-level claim set is what TDX produces.

MAA's role is to evaluate verifier-owned policies (`x-ms-policy-hash`) over those claims and emit an EAT. The policies are written in MAA's policy language and evaluate boolean predicates on measurement values. So MAA does evaluate predicates on attested evidence rather than emitting a binary "trusted / not trusted" - this partially overlaps the paper's predicate-on-coverage shape, but the predicates evaluate over launch-time measurement hashes, not over per-sensor runtime state. The `x-ms-runtime` claim is the closest fit to a runtime-telemetry channel, but it is structurally identical to the SEV-SNP REPORT_DATA approach: the platform signs whatever the enclave puts there, without itself certifying the values as sensor evidence.

## 6. AWS Nitro Enclaves

The AWS Nitro Enclaves attestation document is a COSE_Sign1 (RFC 8152) tagged CBOR object documented in `aws-nitro-enclaves-nsm-api/docs/attestation_process.md`. The payload fields are: `module_id`, `digest` (the hash algorithm used, must be "SHA384"), `timestamp` (UTC milliseconds), `pcrs` (map of 1-32 PCR-index-to-value entries, each 32/48/64 bytes), `certificate` (DER X.509), `cabundle` (CA chain), `public_key` (optional 1-1024 byte enclave-supplied key), `user_data` (optional 0-512 byte protocol-defined data), `nonce` (optional 0-512 byte challenge from the consumer). The PCRs encode the enclave image (PCR0), kernel + bootstrap (PCR1), application data (PCR2), and IAM role binding (PCR8). Trail of Bits' February 2024 note "A few notes on AWS Nitro Enclaves: Images and attestation" documents the convention.

The structural shape is the same as TDX, SEV-SNP, and Apple PCC: a fixed set of hash registers measured at launch, plus a small user-defined slot. The 512-byte `user_data` field is, in principle, large enough to encode a structured sensor-state JSON, but the platform does not interpret it; the AWS Nitro hypervisor signs that the enclave placed those bytes there, not that they represent valid sensor evidence. Per-sensor coverage is not a primitive.

## 7. NVIDIA Hopper / Confidential Computing

The NVIDIA Hopper H100 Confidential Compute whitepaper, WP-11459-001 v1.0, July 25, 2023, describes the attestation report as "containing the measurements of the HW and SW components" of the GPU. The attestation flow: the GPU's Attestation Key (regenerated at every full chip reset, certified by the device-unique Identity Key) signs evidence over the firmware, microcode, and CUDA driver versions. The NVIDIA Remote Attestation Service (NRAS) verifies the evidence against a Reference Integrity Manifest (RIM) of "golden measurements" fetched from the RIM service. NRAS emits an EAT JWT with the attestation result. Conformance is binary at the RIM level (measurements match the published reference set), with optional appraisal-policy overrides via the UAM Rego policy engine.

The attested quantity is GPU firmware and microcode identity, plus the driver version. There is no per-sensor coverage, no `dropped_event_count`, no `deadline_miss_count`, and no installed-vs-never-installed distinction beyond "RIM Bundle Not Found" (which fails the entire attestation). NVIDIA's design is closer to NVIDIA Hopper as a deeply-attested *application* (firmware images on a peripheral) than to a *substrate* of sensors whose runtime health needs to be reported.

## 8. The structural delta

The TEE attestation lineage stops at a structural boundary that the sensor-grounded admission paper crosses by design. Every surveyed wire format encodes evidence as a hash chain of code or firmware images loaded into a trust domain, plus a small fixed user-defined nonce slot that the platform does not interpret. The verifier's predicate is, at most, set-membership against a published reference set (PCC transparency log, NRAS RIM bundle, MAA policy hash, SEV-SNP launch-digest comparison). Runtime measurement registers - TDX RTMR0-3, Arm CCA REM, Nitro PCR8 - record the existence and order of post-launch events as SHA-384 extension chains but do not expose per-component health, drop counts, deadline misses, or installed-vs-never-installed semantics; a degraded sensor and an uninstalled sensor produce indistinguishable extension chains.

The sensor-grounded admission paper's wire format, by contrast, declares a finite enumeration `ProviderKind` and types each provider record as `(id, kind, inst, act, healthy, deg, reasons, drop, miss)`. The verifier's admission predicate `requiredSetCovered` is a structural induction over the required-set map, not a hash comparison; `dropAndMissBounded` is an interval predicate over the integer counters in each provider record, not a digest equality. The wire format separates `inst=⊥` (never installed) from `(inst=⊤, healthy=⊥)` (installed-and-degraded), and the admission verdict distinguishes the two cases. None of the TEE attestation formats surveyed has the schema to express either side of that distinction.

The Confidential Computing Consortium, IEEE, and Intel community publications converge on the framing: "while attestation can verify the platform's trustworthiness, it primarily concentrates on measurements taken during boot or launch time, creating a notable gap in runtime integrity measurement" (Intel developer article on runtime IMA-into-RTMR). The closest existing work to closing this gap (PDRIMA, Keylime, Scanclave, draft-moriarty-rats-posture-assessment-00) all measure code at runtime and extend that into hash registers; none measure sensor-coverage state and emit it as structured claims a verifier evaluates as a predicate. The structural addition the sensor-grounded admission paper makes is on a layer the TEE attestation lineage does not address. TEE attestation answers "what code is running on this host?" The sensor-grounded admission predicate answers "what sensors did this host's runtime confirm were healthy when it produced this receipt?"

A TEE-rooted kernel whose launch attestation verifies and whose sensor-grounded attestation reports a degraded provider is structurally degraded under the paper's admission predicate. The two attestation layers compose: the TEE attestation answers the code-identity question and the sensor-grounded attestation answers the runtime-coverage question. They are not in tension and not substitutes.

## 9. Recommended Section 8 changes

The current Section 8 framing ("adjacent but distinct: TEE attestation reports which code is running; the substrate-state attestation reports which sensors are healthy") survives this deeper investigation intact. The framing should be sharpened, however, with two structural commitments that this survey now grounds:

First, add a `sensor_evidence_absent_in_TEE` bibliography anchor against the published wire formats:

- `\bibitem{intelTDX2023}` Intel Corporation. *Intel Trust Domain Extensions (Intel TDX) Module Base Architecture Specification*. Document 348549-002US, January 2023. Cite Section 12.1.2 (RTMR) and Section 12.2 (TDREPORT) in defense of the claim that RTMR is a SHA-384 extension chain rather than a structured per-sensor telemetry channel.
- `\bibitem{intelTDXDCAP2025}` Intel Corporation. *Intel TDX DCAP Quote Generation Library and Quote Verification Library API*. Rev 0.9, May 2025. Cite Appendix A.3.2 / A.4.4 for the verifier-facing Quote v4 / v5 format.
- `\bibitem{amdSEVSNP2025}` Advanced Micro Devices. *SEV Secure Nested Paging Firmware ABI Specification*. Publication 56860, Revision 1.58, May 2025. Cite Table 21 (`ATTESTATION_REPORT` structure) for the launch-only `MEASUREMENT` field and the 64-byte `REPORT_DATA` user slot.
- `\bibitem{applePCCBundle2024}` Apple Inc. *security-pcc: CloudAttestation/Proto/AttestationBundle.proto*. October 2024. Cite the proto field list (`sep_attestation`, `ap_ticket`, `sealed_hashes`, `provisioning_certificate_chain`, `app_data`, `transparency_proofs`, `local_policy`, `sfr_manifest`) as the canonical PCC attestation surface.
- `\bibitem{armCCA2026}` Ferraiuolo, Mandyam, Faria. *Arm's Confidential Compute Architecture Reference Attestation Token*. draft-ffm-rats-cca-token-03, March 2026. Cite the realm-token claim list (RIM, REM, profile, personalisation value) as the CCA wire format.
- `\bibitem{awsNitroNSM2024}` Amazon Web Services. *aws-nitro-enclaves-nsm-api Attestation Process*. Cite the COSE_Sign1 payload field list (`module_id`, `digest`, `timestamp`, `pcrs`, `certificate`, `cabundle`, `public_key`, `user_data`, `nonce`).
- `\bibitem{nvidiaHCC2023}` Nertney, R. *Confidential Compute on NVIDIA Hopper H100*. Whitepaper WP-11459-001 v1.0, July 2023. Cite the RIM-bundle-matching attestation flow.
- `\bibitem{microsoftMAATDX2026}` Microsoft. *Azure Attestation EAT profile for TDX*. Page version 2026-02-27. Cite the TDX claim list and the `x-ms-runtime` JSON-bag escape hatch.
- `\bibitem{ratsArch2022}` Birkholz, H. et al. *Remote Attestation Procedures Architecture*. RFC 9334, January 2023. Cite Section 4.2 for the Claims-as-name/value-pairs framing and the explicit delegation to lower-level profiles.

Second, replace the current sentence in Section 8 reading "the substrate-state attestation reports which sensors are healthy" with a tighter structural claim that anchors against what the surveyed TEE formats cannot express:

> The TEE attestation lineage attests fixed code and firmware measurements with a small user-defined nonce slot; what the verifier evaluates is, at most, set-membership of the launch digest against a published reference set (the PCC transparency log, the NVIDIA RIM bundle, the MAA policy hash, the SEV-SNP launch comparison). Runtime measurement registers (TDX RTMR, CCA REM, Nitro PCR8) extend SHA-384 hash chains over post-launch events but cannot, by construction, distinguish "sensor degraded mid-decision" from "sensor never installed." The sensor-grounded admission predicate's wire format types each provider with `(installed, active, healthy, degraded, drop, miss)` and evaluates the verifier's required-set as a structural induction over typed records, not as a digest equality. The two layers compose: a TEE-rooted kernel whose launch attestation verifies and whose sensor-grounded attestation reports a degraded provider is structurally degraded under the admission predicate, in a way the underlying TEE attestation does not have the schema to express.

The current `chengTDXDemystified2024`, `amdSEVSNP2020`, `armCCA2023`, `nvidiaHopperCC2023`, `microsoftMAA2025`, and `applePRA2024` citations remain useful as secondary anchors; the new bibliography entries above are the load-bearing primary-source anchors that survive scrutiny from a TEE-attestation-literate reviewer.
