# Wave 1B: Fresh adversarial cold read

Fresh-eye USENIX Security 2027 PC pass on a paper the prior swarm has marked "READY." The prior adversarial review (`proposals/10-final-adversarial-review.md`) closed on "zero substantive findings"; my reading surfaces seven of them, three of which I rate as structural rather than editorial. The local optimum the swarm hit is "the prose matches the proofs and the bib." The optima it missed are placement-of-claim, threat-model precision, and reader-onboarding for a USENIX-only audience.

## 1. Abstract <-> headline theorem promise alignment

The Cycle 3 rename to `admission_predicate_separates_healthy_and_degraded_witnesses` does not in fact match the abstract. The abstract sentence is: "The headline result is the existence of two receipts sharing identical body bytes whose distinct sensor attestations discharge the admission predicate to opposite verdicts." The theorem name says "separates"; the abstract says "exist two ... discharging to opposite verdicts." Those are not synonyms. "Separates" is a universal-quantifier claim (a predicate that separates healthy from degraded *for all* witness pairs); the abstract and the Lean both deliver an *existential*. §4:39 confirms this explicitly: "The empty list suffices as a degraded witness; the stronger separation that the predicate reliably rejects any short-falling attestation is the required-set-coverage projection (Theorem~3)." So the headline name says "separates" but §4 admits it only delivers two witnesses, and §10:6 carries the same softening. A hostile reviewer will read the theorem name first and the §4 caveat second, and conclude the title is overclaimed by one quantifier. The fix is either to demote "separates" in the theorem name to "witnesses" / "exhibits," or to promote the abstract to the stronger universal claim that the proof actually delivers via the required-set-coverage projection. Pick one.

## 2. Threat model: deployed kernel anchor leaves attacker capabilities fuzzy

§6 anchors the empirical chapter on "a working admission kernel," and the introduction calls it "the Chio substrate" (§1:6). No section ever states the attacker model in the form a USENIX reviewer wants: "the attacker is a *who*, has *these* capabilities, and is excluded from *these* operations." The closest statement is §4:68 ("controls the agent process, malformed evidence, and stale treaty references; cannot break the listed cryptographic primitives") plus §7:17 ("a kernel whose attestation lane is compromised at the same time as its sensing lane can sign coherent false claims"). What the paper never says: (a) can the attacker control the kernel's *signing key*? (b) can the attacker control the *host* the kernel runs on but not the key? (c) is the threat model the *defender* of the kernel or the *consumer* of receipts? §1's "the threat model controls the agent, the foreign polity, and the malformed evidence. It does not control the kernel's sensors" reads like the agent and the kernel are on the same side, but §7:17 introduces a "compromised attestation lane" which sounds like a kernel-side attacker. The two paragraphs are not consistent. A USENIX PC will mark this as STFM (state the threat model). Fix: a single §3 or §4 paragraph titled "Adversary model" with three rows (in-scope capabilities, out-of-scope capabilities, separation from prior work).

## 3. §9 single-key collapse: gestures at TEE but ducks the open question

The execution-complete memo says S1 was closed by §9's attestation-key-isolation row plus §3's TEE-rooted separation paragraph. Read together, they do not close it; they reframe it. §9:22 ("The strengthening of this paper rests on operational discipline rather than on cryptographic isolation of the body-signing and attestation-signing roles, which share one key") concedes the present construction has no key isolation, then lists hardware paths that *would* close the gap. §3:21 mirrors this gesture. The reviewer's question is: *under what threat model does TEE isolation actually close the gap?* The paper never engages the TEE-compromise literature. Grep returns zero hits for "side-channel," "Rowhammer," "fault injection," "Plundervolt," "Downfall," "TDX-Down." A 2026 USENIX reviewer reading this section will think: "they're outsourcing the residual to a TEE substrate without admitting that the TEE substrate has its own residual." The §9:22 row should engage at least one TEE-attack family (microarchitectural side channels OR voltage-glitching) and acknowledge that "TEE-rooted attestation" pushes the residual one layer down, not to zero. The Cycle 3 fix was a citation paste; the structural concession the reviewer wants is one sentence saying "TEE substrate compromise reduces sensor-grounded admission to single-key signing for receipts produced during the compromise window."

## 4. §8 TEE coverage: misses NVIDIA CC, GCP, CoCo, Pluton

§8:11 reads as a TEE survey paragraph with Intel TDX, AMD SEV-SNP, AWS Nitro, Apple PCC, Arm CCA. The §2:21 background paragraph adds "NVIDIA Hopper Confidential Compute" via the `hammoudConfidentialCloud2023` umbrella citation, but §8 does not engage NVIDIA-CC's *wire format* (it has GPU attestation reports with measurement extensions). Other absences: Google Cloud Confidential Computing (no hit anywhere in `bib.bib`); CNCF Confidential Containers (no hit); Microsoft Pluton (no hit); Apple Private Cloud Compute's *attestation chain* details beyond the SealedHashLedger sentence in §8:11 (Apple PCC ships node-identity + transparency-log + cryptex-measurement layers that the single sentence elides). For a 2026 USENIX reviewer, this is "the survey covers the X86 TEE families and Apple but stops short of the GPU-attestation generation that is dominating 2026 confidential-compute work." Fix: one sentence each on NVIDIA-CC (per-GPU SP-DM attestation reports), GCP Confidential VMs (SEV-SNP + Confidential Space attestation tokens), and Confidential Containers (CNCF Kata + remote-attestation-service shape). These three were the dominant 2025/2026 systems-security venue topics; their absence reads as "survey written before the GPU TEE wave." Microsoft Pluton is more peripheral; an aside footnote is sufficient.

## 5. Five-mode ladder: first-mention assumes the parent paper

The five modes (observation, guarded, receipt-backed, partition-contingency, quorum-required) are first named in §2:9 inside a parenthetical: "The trust ladder has five ordered modes (observation, guarded, receipt-backed, partition-contingency, quorum-required), and treaty admission is stable under any mode at or above the treaty's mode floor." That is the *only* enumeration. Subsequently §1:10 references "the parent paper's five-mode ladder" without defining the modes; §3:27 says receipt-backed and above require coverage; §5:13 lists four evaluator outcomes; §7:8 references all three. A reader who has not read the parent paper has no idea what "guarded" or "observation" or "quorum-required" mean operationally. §2:9 needs one sentence per mode (one line each, under 80 chars), or a small table. The current single-parenthetical first-mention is a parent-paper-required gate. A USENIX-only audience cannot pass it. This is the biggest reader-onboarding gap I found.

## 6. Zero figures at 18 pages

The paper has no figures, tables, listings, or diagrams. For a USENIX Security paper, this is unusual; for a paper whose central artifact is a *receipt schema* and a *predicate composed of three conjuncts*, it is conspicuous. The top two figure candidates:

(a) §4's three-conjunct admission predicate (`allPredicates` + `requiredSetCovered` + `dropAndMissBounded`) is described in eight lines of inline math at §4:24-31, with the worked example a paragraph of prose at §4:50. A figure showing the predicate evaluator's branching on a healthy vs degraded witness (two columns, three rows for the three conjuncts, with $\top$/$\bot$ verdicts) would replace ~250 words of §4:39 prose and would be the single most-recalled artifact for a reviewer.

(b) §3's sensor-state attestation schema, currently described as five paragraphs of prose at §3:7-16. A box diagram of the canonical-JSON object with its `providers` array, `clock` record, and DSSE subject digest binding (showing how attestation and body share a digest) would replace ~200 words.

A third candidate: §8:11's TEE-wire-format comparison ("Intel TDX exports a build-time MRTD digest, four runtime measurement registers...AMD SEV-SNP issues an `ATTESTATION_REPORT`...AWS Nitro Enclaves emit a COSE_Sign1 document with PCR0-PCR8...") is a four-row TEE-by-wire-format table waiting to be tabular. The prose is dense enough that a reviewer will skim it. A table will be read.

## 7. §7 discussion: drifts into placement defense, not structural argument

§7's three paragraphs are "the structural argument," "why this is not heuristic substitution," and "composition with the parent paper," followed by "what about kernels that lie?" and "what this paper does not claim." The first three are a structural argument; the latter two are defense paragraphs. The placement defense at §7:8 ("A heuristic is a recommendation to a human operator; a predicate is a Boolean over the receipt's substrate-attested fields") is good but reads like §8 material rephrased to anticipate a reviewer's question. §7's missing paragraph is "what does this construction make possible that nothing else does." The closest sentence to that is §3:18's "the paper does not specify the audit channel; it specifies the structural placement that makes the audit channel coherent," but it lives in §3 where the reader has not yet been told what an audit channel would do. §7 should host that argument. Fix: add a paragraph titled "What this construction enables that prior work does not" before §7:13, naming the *downstream* claim that the placement choice unlocks (a sensor-coverage auditor whose disagreement is a signed artifact; a treaty whose required-set predicate is decidable across organizational trust boundaries).

## 8. Voice violations: 14 hits across "this paper" + 1 across "constitutional amendment"

Grep against `"this paper|we extend|v0|v1|wave|iteration|cycle|fixture"` returned **14 hits** for "this paper" / "the construction in this paper" / "the paper" with editorial voice (§1:10, §2:18, §3:18, §5:19, §7:16, §7:17, §9:7, §9:22, §9:28, §10:8, +4 others), and **2 hits** on the README's "no engineering-meta" rule. The README is explicit: "It does not refer to code branches, project versions, internal artifact counts, fixture matrices, or release-engineering steps. Citations to runtime code use `\codepath{...}`." The "this paper" hits are *not* engineering-meta in the strict sense (no branch / version / fixture mention), but they violate the README's "describes what the substrate is" voice rule by repeatedly inserting the authorial agent. §1:10 ("The structural distinction is the contribution"), §3:18 ("The paper does not specify the audit channel"), §7:16 ("The paper does not claim that..."), and §9:28 ("the structural strengthening this paper contributes") are the most flagrant. The README's voice is "the substrate does X" rather than "this paper says the substrate does X." Em-dash grep is clean (zero hits). Project-history grep ("branch|commit|repo") returned no hits in sections, but bib.bib has "open-source repository" in `applePCC2024:280` which is fine for a citation. The voice-violation count is high enough that a careful reader will notice the paper *talking about itself* in places where the README says it should be *describing the substrate*.

## Submission readiness verdict

**Needs a Wave 2 round of fixes for a strong submission.**

The paper is acceptable as-is for a borderline submission (the prior swarm's "READY" assessment is defensible at the level of "no PC will reject this for ineligibility"). For a strong submission (top-third PC ranking), three blockers need closure:

1. **Threat model paragraph (finding #2).** A USENIX reviewer cold-reading §1+§4+§9 cannot reconstruct who the attacker is. One §3 or §4 paragraph, three rows (in-scope, out-of-scope, separation from prior work), would close this. This is the single highest-leverage fix.

2. **Five-mode ladder definitions in §2 (finding #5).** The paper is unreadable for a USENIX-only audience without one line per mode. §2:9 is a 50-word fix that retires a parent-paper dependency.

3. **One or two §4 figures (finding #6) plus the §8 TEE table.** Eighteen pages of prose with no figures will read as a formal-methods paper to a systems-security audience. The §4 three-conjunct evaluator branching figure is the highest-recall artifact this paper can ship; the §8 wire-format table converts dense prose into a scannable claim.

Findings #1 (theorem-name-vs-abstract), #3 (TEE compromise residual), #4 (NVIDIA-CC / GCP / CoCo absence), #7 (§7 placement), and #8 (voice "this paper") are editorial-grade and can be addressed in a Wave 2 polish pass. None of them blocks an Accept verdict; all of them widen the path to a top-third ranking.

Total: 8 findings, 3 structural, 5 editorial. The prior adversarial review's "zero substantive findings" was a local optimum on prose-vs-proof alignment; the gaps were in threat-model precision, reader onboarding, and visual artifacts.
