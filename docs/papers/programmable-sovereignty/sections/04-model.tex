\section{Bounded Formal Model}
\label{sec:model}

The formal development isolates the admission logic from the mechanisms that
construct its inputs. It does not model cryptography, canonical serialization,
clock correctness, network transport, durable storage, or organizational key
custody. Those mechanisms must establish the fields of a bounded receipt view
before any theorem in this section applies.

\subsection{Receipts, Predicates, and Polities}

A receipt view is the tuple
\[
\begin{aligned}
r = (&\mathit{id},\mathit{hash},\mathit{action},
      \mathit{participants},\\
     &\mathit{modeRank},\mathit{liveContinuations},
      \mathit{decision},\\
     &\mathit{failureCode},\mathit{evidenceDigests}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The fields are finite data: strings, natural numbers, lists, and an
allow-or-deny decision. In particular, denial is represented rather than
inferred from the absence of an allow. A predicate can therefore require a
specific denial code or an evidence digest just as it can require an action
class or participant.

The syntax is
\[
 p ::= \top \mid \bot \mid \mathsf{atom}(a)
       \mid p \land p \mid p \lor p \mid \neg p.
\]
Atoms inspect exactly one of the following relations: receipt identity, receipt
hash equality, participant-kernel membership, action-class equality, ladder
rank at least a constant, live-continuation membership, decision equality,
failure-code equality, or evidence-class and digest equality. The interpreter
\(\mathsf{denote}(p,r)\) is total and Boolean. This restriction is
intentional. It gives every production-named atom a concrete meaning without
pretending that implication between arbitrary host-language closures is
decidable.

A constitution \(K=[p_1,\ldots,p_n]\) admits \(r\) when every predicate
does:
\[
  \mathsf{admits}(K,r) = \bigwedge_{p\in K}\mathsf{denote}(p,r).
\]
A polity is the pair \(P=(T,K)\), where \(T\) is a predicate defining the
local receipt-admission scope. Thus
\[
  \mathsf{polityAdmits}(P,r) =
  \mathsf{denote}(T,r)\land\mathsf{admits}(K,r).
\]
The model contains no membership roster, territorial jurisdiction, or
authority over another log. Its subject is one receiver's classification of
receipts at its own boundary.

\subsection{Bilateral Intersection}

A bilateral treaty
\(\tau=(T_\tau,K_\tau,P_L,P_R,f_\tau)\) contains treaty scope and
constitutional predicates, the two participant polities, and a minimum ladder
mode \(f_\tau\). Admission is defined by the grouped expression
\[
\begin{split}
\mathsf{treatyAdmits}(\tau,r) = {}&
 \mathsf{denote}(T_\tau,r)\\
 &{}\land\mathsf{admits}(K_\tau,r)\\
 &{}\land\mathsf{polityAdmits}(P_L,r)\\
 &{}\land\mathsf{polityAdmits}(P_R,r).
\end{split}
\]
\thm{treaty_admission_iff_predicate_intersection} proves that this expression
is equivalent to its fully expanded six-conjunct form. The theorem is
structural, but that is precisely its role: neither participant's scope or
constitution disappears when the predicates are regrouped for evaluation.
It says nothing about whether Rust constructed the intended receipt view or
verified the signatures that bind it.

Mode admission prepends a finite ladder check:
\[
 \mathsf{underMode}(\tau,m,r) =
 \mathsf{atLeast}(m,f_\tau)\land\mathsf{treatyAdmits}(\tau,r).
\]
\thm{treaty_admission_stable_under_ladder_floor} proves that, under the
hypothesis \(\mathsf{atLeast}(m,f_\tau)=\mathsf{true}\), mode admission
reduces to treaty admission. A mode below the floor denies. The theorem is
bounded to the ladder representation imported by the model.

\subsection{Finite-Domain No-Widening}

For constitutions \(K'\), \(K\), and an explicitly declared finite domain
\(D\), define
\[
 \mathsf{Refines}_{D}(K',K)
   \equiv \forall r\in D.\;
   \mathsf{admits}(K',r)\Rightarrow\mathsf{admits}(K,r).
\]
The executable checker evaluates this implication for every member of \(D\).
\thm{refinesOnConstitution_iff} proves that the Boolean result is equivalent
to \(\mathsf{Refines}_{D}\), and
\thm{bridge_decidable_soundness} exposes its soundness direction. A
\codepath{ConstitutionalDelta} carries \(K\), \(K'\), \(D\), and the successful
check as a construction-time witness. The development contains both a
nontrivial narrowing that constructs such a delta and a widening candidate
that evaluates to false.

This property is no-widening on \(D\). It does not establish refinement outside
\(D\), preserve receipts that the old constitution happened to admit, or
justify a claim about all possible future schemas. A narrower constitution can
reject old allows while satisfying the theorem. The current Rust runtime also
has no amendment-enactment path consuming \codepath{ConstitutionalDelta};
amendment is therefore a model-level extension, not a production contribution.

\subsection{Bridges and Assurance Boundary}

The earlier Lean representation used opaque predicates over receipt
identifiers. Given an explicit resolver from an identifier to a
\codepath{ReceiptView}, \codepath{toClosure} lifts a syntactic constitution
into that representation. \thm{bridge_pointwise},
\thm{treaty_admission_agrees}, and
\thm{treaty_admission_under_mode_agrees} prove pointwise agreement after the
lift. They do not turn a finite domain into a universal one.

The Rust predicate evaluator was written independently from the Lean
interpreter. Differential tests apply a mechanical conversion from generated
Rust values into a second Rust specification that mirrors the Lean
definitions. They cover every atom form and 1,024 generated cases each for
compound predicates, constitutional admission, and finite refinement. This is
useful evidence against representation drift. It is neither extraction from
Lean nor a proof of equivalence between Lean and production Rust.
