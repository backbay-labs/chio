The Lean theorem applies to the receipt fields, predicate forms, and finite
domain defined in Section~\ref{sec:model}. It proves the checker correct for
that supplied domain. Completeness of the domain, parsing, canonicalization,
cryptography, clocks, stores, complexity-limit enforcement, and the receiver
hook remain outside the theorem. The differential corpus stays within those
limits and cannot establish universal implementation equivalence.

Two valid signatures establish control of two configured keys. Organizational
independence, honest policy evaluation, uncompromised key custody, and execution
inside an attested kernel require additional deployment controls. HSM-, KMS-,
or TEE-backed keys could strengthen key custody, but we did not evaluate those
configurations.

The performance results come from a deterministic single-host setup with
SQLite. The experiments exclude wide-area transport, mTLS setup, partitions,
concurrent load, large receipt stores, hardware security modules, trusted
execution environments, and crash recovery during continuation consumption.
The sample counts are too small to support operational tail-latency targets.

The 20 negative cases cover named attacks, not an unbounded adversary. Some
failures occur in the envelope verifier before a dispatch-capable runtime
surface exists. The full denial benchmark supplies the signed SQLite receipt
and zero-invocation observation for the complete pre-dispatch path; it should
not be generalized to every internal error in Chio.

Finally, the buyer package establishes internal consistency under its
configured keys. It cannot prove remote process integrity, prevent an
authorized kernel from lying, or compel another organization to accept the
same evidence.
